## Show notes

"Nobody looks at photos." Kate says it with total confidence in the first ten seconds, right before Dan's camera flash goes off in her face. It is Halloween week in the studio, so there are jack-o'-lanterns on the shelf, cobwebs in the corners, and a witch hat on Kate. Dan leads this episode and brings the facts. Kate brings some out-of-date ones.

The question underneath is a real one: do photos and posts on a Google Business Profile actually help? Dan's figures, each one credited as he says it:

- Listings with photos get 42 percent more requests for directions than listings without them, and 35 percent more clicks through to their websites. Source: Google and Oxera, 2014, still quoted in Google's 2026 playbook. It is older data, and it compares photos to no photos at all.
- Adding photos to four business profiles produced no measurable change in map rankings. Source: Sterling Sky, 2021.
- Posting weekly for nine weeks across 441 keywords produced no measurable ranking impact in the local pack. Source: Sterling Sky, 2021.
- In a study of about a thousand Google posts, posts with real photos got 5.6 times more clicks than posts with stock photos. Source: Sterling Sky, 2021.
- Local search experts ranked photo quality number 11 for turning searchers into customers, and post frequency number 148 for map rankings, out of 187 factors. Source: Whitespark, 2026.
- Regularly updated Business Profiles get 5 times more views. Source: Google, 2026.
- Local results are mainly based on relevance, distance, and popularity. Source: Google Business Profile Help, 2026.

So photos and posts are not a ranking cheat code. They are local proof: proof the business is real, and proof it is open. Photos close the deal, because they show people exactly what they will get. Posts open the conversation, because they say the business is here, it is busy, and here is what is new.

You can do this yourself. Or, if your plate is full, 6 String Creative will take it off your hands. For the Google Business Profile, that is filling in every section, setting the categories and services, then adding real photos of the work every month and writing posts about the offers, each with a button to call or book. For Google Maps, that is checking the pin is in the right spot, the name, address, and phone match across the web, and the hours are right, then tracking where the business shows up every month with a plain-English report. Nobody honest can promise the top spot. Pick any piece, or none.

This week's tune-up: open your Google Business Profile and look at your newest photo. Is it from the last three months? Then check your last post. If it is older than your favorite shoes, post one this week with a real photo, a title, and a button.
