## Show notes

You showed up on Google. But did they trust you? Kate brings the review stats. Dan brings eleven reviews, one of them from his aunt, and the certainty that he is fine.

The profile gets a business found. Reviews are what get it chosen. Kate's figures, each one credited as she says it:

- 98 percent of people used the internet to find information about local businesses. Source: BrightLocal, 2023.
- 97 percent of consumers read reviews for local businesses. Source: BrightLocal, 2026.
- Most people formed an opinion after reading one to six reviews. Source: BrightLocal, 2015. That is a throwback, not where people are now.
- 49 percent of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation. Source: BrightLocal, 2026.
- 47 percent of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. Source: BrightLocal, 2026.
- 74 percent of consumers only care about reviews written in the last three months. Source: BrightLocal, 2026.

So it is not "get reviews" and stop. It is "keep getting reviews." The profile and the reviews are one system: the profile says the business is active, and fresh reviews say it is worth trusting.

You can do this yourself. Or, if it is one more thing on a full plate, 6 String Creative will take it on. For the Google Business Profile, that is a cleanup of the name, address, phone, hours, and categories, then fresh photos, posts, and a reply to every review each month. For Google Maps, that is the work Google weighs when someone nearby searches, plus a plain-English report of where the business shows up. For reviews, that is a simple text or email ask right after a visit. Pick one, two, all three, or none.

This week's tune-up: open the business on Google Maps and look at the newest review. If it is older than three months, ask three happy customers from this week.

The [Free Google Profile Snapshot](https://6stringcreative.com) is a second set of eyes on the listing and the reviews, with no pressure.
